About

I am a PhD student in Dr. Barnali Dixon's Geospatial Analytics Lab at the University of South Florida. My work combines GIS, machine learning, and physics-based hydraulic modeling (HEC-RAS) to predict flooding, and it is expanding toward water-quality risk across Florida watersheds.

My path started in the field. I trained as a Geomatic Engineer at Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology in Ghana and spent years on cadastral and engineering surveys with GNSS receivers, total stations, and drones. That background shapes how I model: I care about where the data comes from, how it is labeled, and whether a predicted map agrees with what is on the ground.

I hold an MA in Geography (GIS and Spatial Analysis) from USF and am a certified GIS Professional (GISP).

Published in the Journal of Hydrology, 2026

Where is machine learning flood research happening, and where is it missing?

A systematic review of 100 peer-reviewed machine learning flood prediction studies published between 1990 and 2024, compared against 2,395 flood events in the EM-DAT disaster database. Pluvial floods are now the most common target (44% of studies), ahead of fluvial (40%), compound (9%), and coastal (7%) flooding.

Figures 10, 11, 8, and 4 from Atta Amponsah, C., Dixon, B., Qiang, Y., & Yan, E. Y. (2026), Journal of Hydrology, 678, 136016. © 2026 Elsevier B.V. Select a figure to enlarge it.

Research tracks flood exposure reasonably well in Asia (50% of studies; 1,450 flood events), Europe (22%; 336 events), and North America (17%; 207 events), but Africa (5%; 97 events) and South America (5%; 244 events) are underrepresented. Indonesia recorded 228 flood events yet appeared in only one study. The review also introduces FLOOD-ML, a framework for choosing a model by flood type, data structure, and goal.

Published in Geo-spatial Information Science, 2026

Which machine learning classifier maps land cover best, and does the season matter?

Using Sentinel-2 imagery and spectral indices in Google Earth Engine, I compared six classifiers (k-nearest neighbor, random forest, support vector machine, gradient tree boost, CART, and naive Bayes) across the four seasons of 2024 in Butler County, Ohio.

Figures 1, 2, 7, and 5 from Atta Amponsah et al. (2026), Geo-spatial Information Science, licensed under CC BY 4.0. Select a figure to enlarge it.

k-NN was the most accurate on average (95.8% overall accuracy), closely followed by random forest (95.5%), while naive Bayes trailed at 78.9%. Spring imagery gave the best results across all classifiers, so when the imagery is captured matters almost as much as which algorithm is used.

Under review

Does a flood model trained on one hurricane work on the next one?

Machine learning flood maps often look excellent on the storm they were trained on. This study asks whether that skill carries over to a different hurricane. I built and compared a convolutional neural network, random forest, XGBoost, and two AutoML frameworks for coastal Florida, using a GIS workflow that draws training labels from HEC-RAS hydraulic simulations and field-surveyed high-water marks.

This paper is in peer review, so results aren't shared here yet. They'll be posted once it's published.

Under review at Computers, Environment and Urban Systems

Where should emergency shelters go when flood risk is uneven?

Shelter-location models usually treat every resident's need the same. This study weights demand by FEMA flood hazard across a coastal Florida county and tests reinforcement learning against exact optimization solvers for choosing where shelters should go.

This paper is in peer review, so results aren't shared here yet. They'll be posted once it's published.
